---
title: "Storyboard mode"
description: "Generate a grid of shots or multiple angles in one image, then upscale the frames you keep."
---

Storyboard mode produces a single image divided into a grid, where each cell is a different shot or
a different angle on the same subject.

## Two modes

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Storyboard">
    Each cell is a **different shot** — a sequence. Use it to block out how a scene cuts together.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Multi-angle">
    Each cell is the **same subject from a different angle**. Use it for character turnarounds and
    reference sheets.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

Three layouts, chosen from a dropdown:

| Layout | Cells |
|---|---|
| 2×2 | 4 |
| 3×3 | 9 |
| 4×4 | 16 |

Choosing a layout forces batch size to 1 — the grid is the output.

**Add shot descriptions** is a toggle. With it on, each cell carries a caption describing the shot.

## Upscaling a cell

The grid is one image, so an individual cell is only a fraction of its resolution. When you find a
frame worth keeping, upscale it.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Pick the cell">
    From the storyboard result card.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose a resolution">
    2K or 4K, with an optional detail-enhancement toggle.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Start the upscale">
    WJI crops that cell, uploads it, and regenerates it at the higher resolution.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Cell upscaling requires a specific model to be available on your account. If it is missing, WJI
  tells you rather than silently substituting a different one.
</Note>

## On the canvas

The canvas has its own storyboard tools that go further — one-click 2×2, 3×3, and 4×4 grids from an
existing image, plus a storyboard builder for assembling frames. See
[Image presets and storyboards](/infinite-canvas/image-presets-and-storyboard).

<Tip>
  Storyboard mode is at its best early, when you are deciding how a scene should be covered. Once
  you know the shots you want, generate them individually at full resolution instead — you get more
  control per frame and do not have to upscale afterwards.
</Tip>
